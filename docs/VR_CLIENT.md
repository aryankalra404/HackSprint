# VR client

Unity 6.3 LTS, URP, Meta XR SDK, targeting **Meta Quest 3S** (full VR, no passthrough).
The project lives at `~/My project` on Aryan's Mac. Its `CLAUDE.md` is the detailed, canonical reference for the scene, lighting, build steps and Unity gotchas. This page covers what the rest of the team needs to know.

## Built (as of 10 Oct; everything here is tested in editor Play mode, not yet on the Quest)
- A daytime corporate boardroom (real-world scale) with baked lighting, a city skyline outside the windows, and a Quest-optimised render setup.
- Presenter position at the foot of the table. The slide TV is behind the presenter, and a confidence monitor on the far wall faces them.
- Rocketbox stakeholder avatars (Microsoft, MIT licence) with seated idle animations and facial blendshapes, including visemes for lip sync. Five hand-placed ones remain as the fallback when there's no laptop or the panel is empty.
- Editor tooling for placing avatars, baking, taking snapshots and building to Quest.
- **Live seating (contract 0.6):** the headset follows the laptop's room (`GET /api/room` long poll), finding the laptop over Wi-Fi by UDP broadcast with USB `adb reverse` as the fallback, and seats the panel as it's edited on the web: any of the 11 Rocketbox business avatars in any of the 9 chairs, with a tent card on the table in each person's colour (off by default; switched on from the web). Tested in editor Play mode against the web app (edits arrive within about a second); not yet on the Quest. With no laptop or an empty panel, the original five hand-placed avatars stay.
- `Tools > DryRun > Capture Web Shots` renders the web portraits of all 11 avatars.
- **The session** (contracts 0.10 and 0.13): the panel looks around until the session starts; it starts when the presenter starts speaking (mic level against the room's background), on A with controllers, or from the dashboard (hands have no session gesture, so nobody ends a pitch by accident). Everyone then turns to the presenter (head, neck and eyes, with blinks). A double tap (B), or the dashboard, ends it; when the presenter goes quiet on the last slide, a 3 s countdown on the monitor (talking cancels it) moves to the questions, and the chair's closing ends it (without a briefed panel, the countdown ends it); another double tap (A, or the dashboard's Run it again) goes again with the same panel. If the laptop is unreachable, the headset carries on and keeps sending until it gets through. While live, the panel keeps its attention on the presenter (only the odd look at the slides), on calm breathing and neutral idles, and some of them nod when the presenter pauses after a phrase. Each avatar is fitted to the table chair by chair (measured from its mesh over its whole idle), so hands rest on the table rather than sinking into it. The confidence monitor shows what to do and a timer.

- **Slides** (contract 0.14): `SlideDeck` downloads every slide of the room's `deck` to the headset as soon as it arrives, then shows the current one on the TV behind the presenter and a speaker view on the confidence monitor (this slide large, the next one, the counter, with the session text moved to the right). With hands, a quick pinch (index fingertip to thumb) with the right hand for next, the left hand for back; with controllers, right trigger next, left trigger back (arrow keys in the editor); the headset changes slide at once and tells the laptop. Only the current slide and its neighbours are decoded, so a long deck doesn't fill the headset's memory. Set up by `Tools > DryRun > Room > Set Up Slides`; `Snapshot Slides` photographs both screens. Tested in editor Play mode.
- **Controller-free** (contract 0.16): hand tracking is on alongside the controllers, and the interaction rig draws the tracked hands (no arms), dressed for the boardroom: lit skin instead of Meta's grey see-through hands, and a dark suit sleeve with a white shirt cuff at each wrist that fades out toward the elbow and follows an estimated forearm, so bending the wrist doesn't swing it. The skin tone is one material (`HandSkin.mat`) to match the presenter. `HandInput` reads the plain pinch (index fingertip to thumb): a quick pinch (under half a second) with the right hand is the next slide, with the left hand the one before. A longer pinch does nothing. Tracking jitters while the fingers touch, so a let-go only counts once the fingers stay apart for a moment, and slides change at most once every 0.4 s: one pinch, one slide. Only quick pinches count, to keep the pinching people do while talking from changing slides. (Meta's thumb microgestures were tried first and recognised nothing on the headset.) Picking up a controller switches back, and the confidence monitor's prompts follow. Set up by `Tools > DryRun > Room > Set Up Hands`; `Snapshot Hands` renders the dressed hands in the editor. Compiles and runs in the editor; the gestures themselves, and how the hands look when tracked, need a Quest build.
- **Eye height** (contract 0.16): `HeightOffset` moves the rig's tracking space by the room's `height_offset`, set with the height bar on the practice page and dashboard, easing to it. Tested in editor Play mode against the web app. Since 0.36 it also moves it sideways by `side_offset` (the left-right bar beside it, along the rig's right), to line up with the seat while sitting; compiles, not yet run on the Quest.
- **Headset view** (contract 0.11): `HeadsetView` (from the headset-stream kit) renders a mono 1280×720 view at the presenter's eye, with the sky and the headset's colour grade, encodes it as H.264 on the Quest's encoder and streams it to the laptop's relay on port 3001, only while the dashboard's headset view is open. `HeadsetStreamLink` points it at the laptop RoomSync found. Set up by `Tools > DryRun > Room > Set Up Headset View`. Not yet tested on the Quest (only an Android build streams).

## To build
1. **Test on the Quest** (built; editor-tested only): live seating (`npm run quest` in `web/`, or Wi-Fi on a shared hotspot, then edit the panel on the practice page), slides with the pinches and the triggers, the height bar, voice start in a noisy room, Wi-Fi discovery and the headset view's frame rate. Later: fade people in and out instead of popping.
2. **Web portraits:** `Tools > DryRun > Capture Web Shots` renders head shots for the web client. Re-run it when avatars change.
3. **Turns (built, editor-tested):** the headset records each moment (the opening, each slide, answers, the pitch's end) and posts it to the laptop's `/api/turn`. A slide goes whole when the presenter moves on, however long it took, and mid-slide only at an interruption point n8n planned (contract 0.25). With a pitch length, the chair keeps time from lines voiced in prep (a nudge, and in a strict meeting stopping the pitch for questions), and the monitor's clock shows the pitch against its limit, amber when close and red past it, with a label. A reaction plays from that person's head with lip sync from its loudness (the head and brows move with the voice), after a raised hand if n8n asks for one, at the presenter's pause; whoever speaks looks at the presenter and the others look at them. The mic is muted while the panel speaks; late reactions are dropped; the last slide hands over to the questions and the chair closes the meeting. *(0.34)* An answer you carry on after a pause to think goes again whole and the panel's reaction to the half of it is dropped; one question waits for its answer at a time; a hand you talk past for 6 s cuts in at your next breath; near the end of the pitch a pause lets n8n hear "that's all from me" and the chair opens the floor; the chair's spoken rescue comes at most once per slide; the last slide's wrap-up waits 3 s and counts down 2. *(0.35)* The chair's clock (and the monitor's, with a limit) stops while a question during the pitch is asked and answered; on the last slide there's no nudge or "time's up": past the cut the pitch just ends at your next pause. Compiles; 0.34 ran on the Quest on 17 Oct, 0.35 not yet. Still to test on the Quest and against n8n.
4. **A panel that feels real (contract 0.26, built 12 Oct, compiles; not yet on the Quest):** the asker's filler ("Mm-hm") plays the moment an answer goes, and the chair's "Thank you" at the pitch's end, from generic lines n8n voices once per voice and the laptop caches. An answer now ends after 0.8 s of quiet (was 1.2 s; the scene's `TurnTaker` was updated too). Park, move-on and hand-back reactions play like any other.
5. **Rescue Agent (built 12 Oct, compiles):** silence during the pitch, with the panel idle, shows the slide's calm, keyword and bridge cues on the confidence monitor in turn (n8n's `rescue_after_s`); at the keyword cue the chair says "Take your time", and a few seconds later asks that slide's way-back-in question, whose answer goes to n8n as an answer. All from the bundle, nothing sent while you're frozen.
6. **Telemetry (built 12 Oct, compiles):** `HeadsetTelemetry` (on the Room object) counts how long the presenter's head faced each person (`gaze_dwell_s`, head direction, shown as "Who you faced") and the slides behind them (`facing_slides_s`), with `TurnTaker`'s silences and rescues; `SessionController` posts it to `/api/debrief` just before it ends the session.
7. **A thinking look** while a turn is on its way (n8n takes 1–4 s).

## What only VR can measure
Gaze distribution (did you ignore Procurement?), stage movement and gestures. These are optional fields in the contract and help make the case for VR over the web client.
